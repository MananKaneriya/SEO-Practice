Lead Scoring Explained: How to Sort Hot, Warm and Cold Leads

By Ortheon AI · Published 24 November 2026 · 5 min read

Lead scoring is a simple way to decide which enquiry deserves your next phone call. Instead of replying in the order that messages arrived, you give each lead a score based on how likely they are to buy soon, and you start with the highest. This guide explains hot, warm and cold leads, gives you a scoring sheet you can copy, and shows how to use the scores every day.

What Lead Scoring Means

Every sales team has more enquiries than time. A buyer who says "I want to see the flat this Saturday" and a person who sends one message asking for a brochure are both leads, but they are not equal today. Lead scoring gives you a quick, fair way to tell them apart so that the buyer who is ready does not wait behind the person who is only browsing.

A score is just a number you add up from what you know about the lead. Most teams then group the numbers into three labels, hot, warm and cold, because three labels are easy for everyone to remember and use.

Hot, Warm and Cold Leads

LabelWhat it usually looks likeWhat to do
HotAsked for a quotation, demo or site visit, and gave a clear time frameCall the same day, ideally within the hour
WarmInterested and asking questions, but has not set a date or a next stepFollow up within two or three days with useful information
ColdOnly browsing, vague about the need, or silent after earlier follow-upsKeep on a monthly list and try again later with a new reason to talk

A Scoring Sheet You Can Copy

Here is a simple sheet that a small team can start with. The points are an example. Change them to match what your own best customers did before they bought.

SignalPoints
Asked for a quotation, demo or site visit+30
Said they plan to buy within 30 days+25
Explained their requirement or budget clearly+15
Replied to your last follow-up+15
Came from a referral or is a repeat customer+10
Only asked a general question+5
No reply to your last two follow-ups−20

Then group the total: 60 or more is hot, 30 to 59 is warm, and below 30 is cold. Write the score and the label next to each lead, and change them whenever something new happens.

How to Use the Scores Every Day

  1. Start with the hot list. Call every hot lead before you look at anything else.
  2. Move to the warm list. Send each warm lead something useful, such as an answer to their question, a brochure or a suggested time to talk.
  3. Give every lead a next-action date. A lead without a date is a lead that will be forgotten.
  4. Update the score. If a lead replies, books a visit or goes silent, change the points straight away.
  5. Review the cold list once a month. Cold does not mean dead. Many cold leads become warm when the timing changes.

Common Mistakes

  • Too many signals. If the sheet has twenty rows, nobody will fill it in. Five to seven signals are enough.
  • Never updating scores. A score from last month is a guess. Update it when the lead's situation changes.
  • Ignoring cold leads completely. Keep a light monthly routine so that they are not lost.
  • Never checking the result. Every quarter, look at the leads that became customers and ask whether your scoring would have marked them hot.

FAQs – Lead Scoring

What is the difference between a hot lead and a warm lead?
A hot lead has shown clear buying signals, such as asking for a quotation or a site visit and giving a date. A warm lead is interested and asking questions but has not committed to a next step or a date.
How many of my leads should be hot?
There is no fixed number. If most of your leads are hot, your scoring is too generous and the label no longer helps your team choose. If none are hot for weeks, check that your scoring reflects what your real buyers did before they bought.
Can I do lead scoring in a spreadsheet?
Yes. A spreadsheet with one row per lead, a score column and a next-action date works well for a small team. Software becomes useful when leads arrive from many channels and nobody has time to update the sheet by hand.
